What is academic
publication?

Research becomes a publication when it passes peer review and appears in a conference proceedings or a scholarly journal. Here is how each one works, starting with conferences.

Conference papers

What is a conference paper?

A short paper, usually 4 to 10 pages, presented at an academic conference and published in its proceedings. Conferences are the main venue in computer science and engineering because they move faster than journals.

How it works

Call for papers1 Submission2 Peer review3 Camera-ready4 Proceedings5
Review time4 to 8 weeks
Submission to proceedingsTypically 3 to 6 months
Usual length4 to 10 pages

Conference categories

A

Type A · Highly competitive

Flagship conferences in a field. Strict review and low acceptance rates, often around a quarter of submissions or fewer.

B

Type B · Established

Reputable conferences with solid peer review, usually published by IEEE, Springer or ACM and indexed in Scopus.

C

Type C · Emerging

Newer or regional conferences. A practical first venue, but quality and indexing vary widely.

These are general categories, not an official system. In computer science, the CORE ranking (A*, A, B, C) is a common reference. Always check a conference's publisher and indexing before you submit.

Journal papers

What is a journal paper?

A full research article, often 8 to 30 pages, published in a scholarly journal after peer review. Journals allow more depth than conferences and are the main venue in most fields.

How it works

Paper
Editor check Peer review Revision Published
Peer reviewTwo or more experts assess the method, results and claims
RevisionMinor or major changes, answered point by point
TimelineTypically 3 to 12 months, sometimes longer

How journals are ranked

Within each subject area, journals are split into four quartiles by citation impact, using Scimago (SJR) or Clarivate (JCR).

Abstract

A Hierarchical Cascade Network with Adaptive Cross-Modal Fusion for Polyp Segmentation and Classification in Colonoscopy Images

Polyps vary widely in size, shape and color, and many look almost the same as the mucosa around them. Convolutional networks keep local detail but see little global context, and transformers do the reverse. HieraCascadeNet fuses a Swin-Base transformer and an EfficientNet-B4 network at four scales through a learnable Cross-Modal Fusion Gate, decodes the fused pyramid with a cascade attention U-Net, and uses one encoder for both segmentation and mask-guided classification. On Kvasir-SEG it reaches a per-image Dice of 0.9142 and an IoU of 0.8614, and on a three-class Kvasir v2 task it reaches 98.4% accuracy, with 25.3 million parameters at 44.2 frames per second.

Abstract

A Controlled Evaluation of Estimators and Selection Rules for Component Attribution in Transformer Model Editing

Transformer model editing begins by choosing which components to change, usually by ranking them with an attribution estimator and then applying a selection rule. This paper compares common estimators and selection rules under one controlled protocol, with the same models, budgets and evaluation, and measures how often each choice picks components whose editing actually changes the target behaviour. Because published studies differ in models, budgets and metrics, it is hard to tell whether a reported gain comes from the estimator, the selection rule or the setup. Holding these fixed separates their effects.

Abstract

Ablation-Rate Transfer Failure in Component Attribution: A Structurally Invisible Aggregate Term

Component attribution often uses a linear surrogate over random component ablations at one ablation rate, then performs worse at other rates. This paper shows most of that drop is not caused by linearity: refitting at the evaluation rate reaches rho = 0.949, while only 26% of the alpha = 0.20 degradation remains. It also shows fixed-cardinality sampling hides aggregate non-additivity, and a pooled-rate model with one scalar parameter reduces out-of-distribution error 11.6x across CIFAR-100 and ViT-Tiny tests.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers about publishing with us.

What does Research and Scholarly Support do?

Research and Scholarly Support is a research supervision team based in Bangladesh that works with students and early-career researchers worldwide. We supervise research studies, turn finished theses into conference and journal papers, and guide manuscripts through submission and revision. Research supervised by our team has produced 21 published papers, including 12 in Elsevier journals and 8 on IEEE Xplore.

Can you help me publish my thesis as a journal or conference paper?

Yes. Send us your completed thesis or dissertation. We review which chapters can become a paper, shortlist suitable journals or conferences, and supervise while you condense, restructure, edit and format the work. You stay the author of your own research, and acceptance is decided by peer review.

How does joining a ready-to-submit project work?

Some of our IEEE conference papers are at the final stage and still have open author positions, listed on each project card. You contribute to the remaining work on the paper under supervision, and authorship is credited according to your actual contribution and the venue's authorship policy. Booking a position is free, and payment is due only if the paper is accepted.

Do I need to pay in advance?

No. There is no advance payment. Supervision fees are agreed in writing and paid only after the paper is accepted, by bKash, Nagad, Rocket, bank transfer, or card through SSLCommerz.

Do you guarantee publication?

No. We guide every stage, but the journal or conference makes the final decision through peer review. Be cautious of anyone who guarantees acceptance.

Which research areas do you supervise?

Medical image analysis, clinical and health data science, biomedical signal processing, mental health and social analytics, computer vision and edge AI, explainable AI, natural language processing, environment, agriculture and remote sensing, and forecasting and reliability.

Which journals and conferences do you target?

IEEE conferences indexed in IEEE Xplore and Scopus, and Q1 journals from publishers such as Elsevier. Current projects target ICMLANT 2026, an IEEE conference, and the Elsevier journals Information Sciences and Biomedical Signal Processing and Control.

Can international students work with you, including students in China?

Yes. We work with students in Bangladesh and abroad, and this site is available in Simplified Chinese. From China, email is the best way to reach us, since WhatsApp is blocked there.
